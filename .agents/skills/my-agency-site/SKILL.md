# My Agency Site

Version 2.0.0. Use this standard whenever building or editing the student's own agency website. Keep one dependable design across industries; personalise the business information, branding and example. No extra design tools, paid media or cinematic scrolling are required.

This skill supplies the own-agency layout instead of Website Builder Standard. Keep the existing Superagent, business setup and course flow. For an explicitly requested premium upgrade, use the installed premium skill for the enhancement while retaining the real business information and clear enquiry path.

## Start with their business

Read the saved service (`lane`), business name, niche (`my_niche` or `niche`), service area, `brand_look`, logo and contact details. Use `my-business` if it already exists; otherwise use the facts collected so far. If records conflict, use the latest explicit student decision. Ask only for genuinely missing information that prevents an honest build. Never copy another student's identity or example contact details.

The site sells the student's service to their chosen industry. A student selling websites to plumbers has a web-design agency website with a plumbing example, not a website offering plumbing services. Do not add services they do not sell. Keep invoice and bank details out of the website.

## Consistent visual direction

Use the same clean studio composition across the service lanes:

- White or very light neutral canvas, dark readable text, one strong brand accent. Use their saved colours thoughtfully; correct unreadable combinations. If no look is saved, use white, #15171D and #2855F6 with DM Sans or a comparable clean sans-serif.
- Clear typography and generous but purposeful spacing. Starting values: content width about 1200px, desktop gutters 40–60px, phone gutters 20px, body text 16–18px, desktop headline 56–72px and phone headline 36–44px. Adjust for the actual words and viewport; do not clip text or shrink it to fit a rigid box.
- Compact header with a simple wordmark, two or three section links and one enquiry action.
- Desktop hero: concise offer on the left, a large relevant service demonstration on the right. Give the demonstration roughly half the available width. It is a main part of the design, not a tiny thumbnail after several paragraphs.
- Phone hero: offer, action, then demonstration, with a small gap. No forced full-screen text block that pushes the example far down the page.
- One filled primary button style, with quieter text links for examples and navigation. Use thin dividers for lists. Alternate open white sections with one subtle tinted example or personal section. A stronger accent closing section is fine.
- Avoid a page made entirely of identical rounded cards. No stock handshakes, fake dashboards, decorative robots, unrelated laptop photographs, particles or loading intros. Keep normal scrolling. Optional motion must leave the page useful with reduced motion.

Use these choices consistently; do not invent a new visual system because the customer's industry changed. Respect a student's explicit design choices. When editing copy or contact details, preserve the existing composition unless the requested change needs a layout adjustment.

## The page

One main agency page. Header links scroll to its sections; genuine example links may open a separate demo.

1. **Hero:** clear service/offer headline, one supporting paragraph, one primary enquiry action and the demonstration. Niche and location can be a short eyebrow or supporting line. Do not force “city + business type:” into every headline.
2. **Example:** one good example shown large, with a short explanation and a truthful label. If the hero already explains it fully, keep this section brief rather than repeating it.
3. **What you get and how it works:** concrete inclusions for their one service, then three short customer-facing steps. Use a ruled list or simple columns rather than repeated card grids.
4. **About:** real person, real role, direct working relationship. Use “I” for a solo operator. A real portrait is optional; a wordmark or initials is fine.
5. **Questions:** three to five practical questions about materials, scope, timing and next steps. Answers reflect confirmed terms.
6. **Contact:** repeat the meaningful action and provide a real contact route. Use a connected form or an honest email link. Never simulate a successful submission. End with a compact identity/contact footer.

The course builds this in stages. Begin with the complete visual composition and available real draft copy, then refine the named sections as the student progresses. Preserve that design through later offer and branding steps. Remove unfinished placeholders before presenting a finished site.

When course messages say “What we do”, update the inclusions section; when they say “Proof”, update the example section. Do not add duplicate sections for those older labels.

## Adapt the demonstration to the service and industry

| Service | Useful demonstration |
| --- | --- |
| Websites | Screenshot of an actual built website, with a matching mobile view where available. Link to the real demo if accessible. Use a relevant niche: a cafe example for cafes, a trade example for trades. |
| AI receptionists | Actual sample call with a matching transcript, or an explicitly labelled “Example conversation” if no recording exists. Explain only the capabilities the student can provide. No fake audio player. |
| Property walkthroughs | Authorised sample video and source stills. State that generated motion comes from photos. If no video exists yet, use labelled source stills and explain that the video example is pending; do not add a fake play button. |

Use the student's available work or authorised course examples. Label fictional businesses “Concept project”; label supplied work “Course demonstration”. Neither is a client commission. Do not claim a course example as the student's own completed work. Never disguise an unrelated example by changing its caption.

If a website sample is missing, create a small relevant fictional demo using available authorised assets when feasible within the build. Otherwise use an honest available demonstration and explain the remaining gap. Do not invent a client, borrow competitor work, require paid generation or claim unavailable assets exist. Do not use onboarding brand logos as client credentials.

No empty proof slots. No testimonials, client logos, experience claims or results until real evidence exists. Replace concept examples with genuine work as the student progresses.

## Copy

- No em dashes. Plain language, short sentences, no “your partner in”, “leverage”, “seamless”, “unlock” or vague “digital solutions”.
- The supporting paragraph adds what happens next and what the buyer supplies. It does not repeat the headline.
- Name the service clearly. Mention AI when it explains an AI receptionist or generated walkthrough; leave unnecessary builder/tool names out.
- Use confirmed delivery times only. Distinguish a first draft from a tested, published result. Do not automatically promise today, tomorrow or a particular business outcome.
- Name the next step on the button: “Get my first draft”, “Ask about a demo” or another accurate action. Avoid “Fill in the contact form”. A quieter example link is fine.
- Keep prices off the page under the current course offer. Do not invent “free”, guarantees or commitment terms.

Example for a website seller whose confirmed offer includes preview before commitment:

> Websites for Sydney businesses
>
> See your new website before you commit.
>
> Send me your business details and photos. See a first draft, then decide whether to go ahead.
>
> Get my first draft

Use their actual area and offer. This is a copy pattern, not text to copy onto every student's website.

## Finish check

Inspect the actual rendered page on desktop and at 390px phone width; check 360px when available. Look at the whole page, not just its HTML. Check readable type and contrast, no overflow or clipping, sensible image crops, an early relevant demonstration and consistent styling through the lower sections.

Try navigation, mobile menu, example links, FAQ controls and media. Check the real enquiry destination. Form delivery is verified only when submission and receipt are confirmed; a mailto link opens an email draft and does not send it. Remove placeholder text, empty proof slots and unsupported claims.

Fix observed issues with focused edits, preserving the layout. Report the working preview and any missing assets or unverified checks briefly. Never claim a browser inspection or delivery test you could not perform. Save the site address and design choices in the existing business record so future edits remain consistent.
